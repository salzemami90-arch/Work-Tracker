01

Remember the work behind the status.

The problem

Progress is scattered across conversations and notes. Plans evolve, new work appears, and returning to a project means reconstructing where it stopped.

The distinctive value

Keep the current plan and its history together: what changed, what was completed, when it happened, why the plan moved, and what comes next.

02

A simple loop that preserves context

1

Open a project

Read the latest summary and next action.

2

Plan the work

Write goals and steps, or paste prepared points.

3

Record and adapt

Complete work, capture discoveries, and explain changes.

4

Return with context

See the current plan and the history that led to it.

Manual entry is a complete workflow. An assistant name is optional; the first version does not read conversations or turn arbitrary text into tasks automatically.

03

Built around varied, ongoing work

Business paperworkSmall-business projectsSoftwareCompetition deadlinesIndependent work

For a person managing several kinds of work who needs a clear overview, dated achievements, and a reliable place to resume. Completing one goal does not close the project.

04

A focused first version

In the proof of concept
  • Projects, goals, flexible steps, and next actions.
  • Dated achievements, summaries, notes, and change history.
  • A compact overview of work and recorded deadlines.
  • Paused projects with preserved context.
Later
  • Automatic collection and summarization of assistant sessions.
  • Further capabilities informed by real use.
Outside the scope
  • Doing the tracked work or publishing other applications.
  • Contacting suppliers or performing transactions.
  • Automatically analyzing pasted text.
05

What a working demonstration must show

1

Create

A fictional project, goal, and steps.

2

Complete

A dated step achievement while the goal remains active.

3

Adapt

New work added with a reason, without erasing progress.

4

Resume

A clear latest state, remaining work, and next action.

Demonstrate the same workflow without requiring any assistant metadata.

06

Delivery and approval

The scope is approved. Product behavior is defined in the PRD; implementation is proposed in the technical specification.

The course submission requires a short demo video and a public source repository. Deployment is optional. Commit, push, and publication require separate authorization. Personal learning notes and real working data stay outside public delivery.

These English pages explain the plan; they are not evidence that the application is already built.
